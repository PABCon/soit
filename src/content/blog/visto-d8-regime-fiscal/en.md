Working remotely for a foreign company while living in Portugal isn't a niche situation in IT anymore. Portugal's D8 visa, its "digital nomad visa," was built exactly for this. But there are two questions almost nobody answers clearly: how much do you actually need to earn to qualify, and how is that foreign income really taxed once you're here?

**One note before we start**: this isn't tax advice. It's an honest summary of what we found from real sources, with a clear caveat where the answer still has a genuine gray area. For your specific case, talk to an accountant.

## The minimum income requirement

For 2026, the D8 visa's income requirement is 4 times Portugal's minimum wage (RMMG). With the 2026 minimum wage set at €920 (Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025), that comes out to **€3,680 a month**.

This isn't a one-time snapshot: Portugal's Ministry of Foreign Affairs requires it to be a sustained average over the three months before you apply, not a single good month. The visa's own legal basis is Law 18/2022, which added Article 61-B to Law 23/2007 (the Foreigners Law).

## The question most people answer wrong: what about IFICI?

If you've already looked at our [net salary calculator](/salario-liquido), you know IFICI (the regime that replaced the old NHR, a flat 20% rate) sits there as an option. The common assumption is: "if my income is foreign, IFICI doesn't apply, so I just get taxed normally."

We checked this properly, and the real answer is different, and better, than that assumption.

The mechanism that actually matters here isn't IFICI's own 20% rate directly. It's Article 81, paragraph 4 of the Portuguese Tax Code (CIRS), a rule originally written for the old NHR regime that's been explicitly extended to IFICI beneficiaries. It applies the **exemption method** to foreign-source income in Categories A (dependent employment) and B (self-employment/freelance), among others.

In practice: if you're working remotely for a foreign client or employer, with genuinely foreign-source income, while holding IFICI status, that income tends to be **exempt from Portuguese income tax entirely**, not taxed at IFICI's 20%, and not taxed at the normal brackets either. "Exempt with progressivity" means that even though it's exempt, it still counts toward determining the tax rate applied to any other Portuguese-source income you have. IFICI's flat 20% rate specifically applies to **Portuguese-source** income from the qualifying activity, a different case from working remotely from Portugal for a foreign entity.

### The honest caveat

There's one point we couldn't confirm with full certainty: the old NHR regime required, for this exemption, either a double-tax treaty that gave the other country the right to tax the income, or, without a treaty, that the income was actually taxable there. It's very likely this condition carries over, since IFICI explicitly invokes the same Article 81, but we didn't find a document confirming this unambiguously for IFICI specifically.

So the honest way to say it is: generally exempt, subject to the usual double-taxation conditions, confirm your specific situation with an accountant before assuming it applies without exception.

## What this means in practice

If you're considering the D8 visa to work remotely in IT from Portugal:

1. Confirm your average income over the last three months actually hits €3,680/month.
2. Don't assume your foreign income will be taxed at the normal brackets just because it's "not eligible for IFICI" in the 20%-rate sense, the Article 81 exemption might already give you a better outcome.
3. Confirm the specific double-taxation conditions for your home country before deciding.
