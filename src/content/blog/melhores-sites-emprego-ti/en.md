If you work in IT in Portugal and you're job hunting, you've probably already bounced between half a dozen different platforms without being quite sure which ones are actually worth your time. This isn't a generic listicle — it's the result of looking directly at each platform, what they actually publish, and specifically, what they don't tell you until after you've already applied.

## The context nobody's talking about: Portugal's pay transparency law is late

Before getting into the platforms themselves, it's worth understanding why this matters right now, not just in the abstract.

The EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU 2023/970) had a transposition deadline into Portuguese law of **June 7, 2026**. Portugal missed it. The government published only a partial draft law (Proposta de Lei n.º 5573) on August 5, 2026; the public consultation closed August 25; the final law **still hasn't passed**. The European Commission is reportedly evaluating infringement proceedings against Portugal over the delay. Separately, an estimated **62% of Portuguese companies** hadn't even started preparing for compliance.

One important clarification, so as not to overstate what the law actually requires: based on the current draft text, the obligation appears to be disclosing salary to the candidate **before the contract is signed** — not necessarily publishing the figure in the job ad itself. That's a real distinction. A separate, more settled provision bans recruiters from asking candidates about their previous salary.

In other words: salary transparency isn't yet the law in Portugal — which goes a long way toward explaining why so few platforms practice it on their own initiative. We surveyed 109 IT professionals in Portugal and 74% said the lack of salary transparency is the single biggest reason they'd abandon a job platform. The market is already ahead of the law; most platforms aren't.

## The platforms, one by one

### itjobs.pt

Portugal's longest-running IT-dedicated job board — over 10 years in the market. Beyond listings (around 800 active at the time of writing), it also runs training courses and industry events, giving it a community dimension few other platforms offer. On salary transparency, it's the exception that proves the rule: the site's own "top salaries" page lists only around 25 jobs with a published salary out of roughly 800 — meaning the overwhelming majority of listings don't show a figure.

**Best for:** anyone who specifically wants IT roles, without the noise of a general-purpose board, and values the community/events angle.

### landing.jobs

A tech-focused platform claiming reach of 250,000+ candidates (local and international). Its pricing model is different from most — no flat per-post fee, instead a success fee on the hire (reportedly around 11% of the first year's gross salary, per public sources). It also runs its own junior-hiring initiative ("Future.Talent") and broader recruitment/RPO services, not just job posting. On salary: a direct check of live listings shows only a fraction display a real range — most don't.

**Best for:** companies wanting a fuller recruitment service, not just a listing; candidates who value international reach.

### Net-Empregos

Portugal's largest general-purpose job board — not IT-specific, but the scale is real: roughly 58,900 active listings, 5.6 million registered candidates, and over 320,000 registered companies by the site's own numbers. Basic posting is free, with paid premium features (no public pricing). For IT specifically, it's a small slice of a much larger universe — useful for volume, not for specialization.

**Best for:** maximizing reach with a general audience; less useful if you want to filter strictly for IT.

### Sapo Emprego

A general-purpose portal under Altice/SAPO, with 15,000+ listings nationally, of which roughly 1,338 fall under the "Informática" category specifically. Like Net-Empregos, it's not a tech-specialized platform — IT is one category among many on a general channel.

**Best for:** candidates already using the SAPO ecosystem; not the first place to check if you're specifically after IT.

### LinkedIn

Not Portugal-specific or IT-specific, but the aggregate volume of tech roles posted by companies in Portugal is significant — practically unavoidable for senior roles or international companies. The known downside: it's a general professional network, not a platform built for fast, IT-specific applications, and salary transparency depends entirely on each company choosing to show it (most don't).

**Best for:** networking and senior/international roles; less efficient for a focused, fast search.

### Indeed Portugal

An aggregator — it pulls listings from many different sources, including company sites directly. High volume (thousands of IT listings nationally), but precisely because it's an aggregator, the quality and consistency of the information (salary included) varies a lot depending on the original source.

**Best for:** sweeping a large volume of sources at once; expect inconsistent quality.

### Glassdoor

Around 52,000 listings in Portugal (general-purpose, not IT-specific). One important clarification worth making: the salary figures shown on Glassdoor are often a **"Glassdoor Estimate"** — an algorithmically calculated value — rather than a number the employer actually disclosed. That's a meaningfully different kind of "transparency" than a platform requiring a real, employer-stated figure.

**Best for:** company culture research and reviews; treat salary figures as estimates, not confirmed facts.

Transparency isn't the only thing that matters when picking a platform — reach, volume, and specialization count too, which is why this comparison tries to be fair to each one of them. ## Practical takeaway

No single platform covers everything. In practice, most IT professionals in Portugal end up using two or three in parallel — a high-volume general board (Net-Empregos, Indeed), a tech-specialized one (itjobs.pt, landing.jobs, Just IT), and LinkedIn for networking and senior roles. While the pay transparency law is still pending, it's worth checking directly which ones actually show salary before investing time in an application — which is exactly what this comparison is for.
