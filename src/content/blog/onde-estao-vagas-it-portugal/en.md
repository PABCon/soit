If you're thinking about IT work in Portugal, there's a question that rarely gets a real-numbers answer: where do the jobs actually are? Not "where we'd like them to be" or "where the company's head office is on paper," but where IT roles are actually posted.

Teamlyzer analyzed 8,730 real tech job postings in Portugal over a 12-month window, and the numbers, published in April 2026, are clear: over 80% of them sit in just two districts.

## The concentration, district by district

![IT job postings by region](/blog/vagas-it-regiao-en.svg)

Lisbon and Porto together account for 82.16% of every IT posting analyzed. The rest of the country, the other 16 districts plus the two autonomous regions, splits the remaining 17.84%. Braga is the third most relevant region at 6.53%, followed by Aveiro (4.64%) and Coimbra (4.41%). From there, the numbers get thin: Setúbal at 1.29%, and everything else, Évora, Faro, the Azores, Madeira, and the remaining inland districts combined, doesn't even add up to 2%.

That doesn't mean there are no jobs outside Lisbon and Porto. It means if you're applying outside those two areas, you're competing for a much smaller slice of the market, and a remote role stops caring where the company is headquartered at all.

## What the market is actually promising next quarter

Experis (ManpowerGroup), in its quarterly survey of 4,258 technology employers across 42 countries, put Portugal among Europe's strongest hiring forecasts for Q4 2026: a Net Employment Outlook of 36% specifically for the Technology & IT Services sector, named alongside Israel as joint EMEA leaders.

Worth being precise here, since a similar-looking number creates real confusion: Portugal's overall Net Employment Outlook, across the whole economy including sectors outside IT, sits at 32%. That's a different number from the 36% that's specific to Technology & IT Services, and it's easy to mix the two up if you don't check the source closely.

Globally (not specifically Portuguese), the same survey names AI Modeling & App Development (34%), AI Literacy (30%), and traditional IT & data skills excluding AI (29%) as the most in-demand areas. Those are global figures, not a confirmed breakdown of the Portuguese market specifically, but they're an honest signal of where demand is actually heading.

## What this means in practice

If you're job hunting in Portugal, the math is simple: the overwhelming majority of real postings sit in Lisbon, Porto, or are remote. Outside those, the market is thin.
