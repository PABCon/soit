Before building Just IT, we did something most job boards skip: we asked people actually applying to IT jobs in Portugal what genuinely bothers them. Not a 1,000-person panel run by an agency — a direct survey, shared across IT communities and our own network, that collected 109 responses from people working in Portugal.

It's not a perfect sample and we're not pretending it is. It skews toward more experienced people (61% have 10+ years in the field) and toward Lisbon and Porto (77% combined). But it's real, and a few of the results surprised us — including one that directly cuts against an assumption we had about our own business model.

## Who answered

Most respondents (61%) are employed but open to opportunities — not desperate people applying to everything, but selective people who'll only move for something that's actually worth it. Only 12% were actively job hunting at the time. That matters: most of the IT talent market in Portugal isn't actively browsing job boards every day. It's passive, waiting for a good enough reason to look.

The dominant profiles are software development (59%) and DevOps/cloud/infrastructure (21%), with smaller groups in cybersecurity, data, QA and product. 50% are based in the Lisbon area, 27% in Porto.

## Where people actually land jobs

We asked where they'd found their last two jobs or contracts. The result leaves little room for doubt:

- **84%** — LinkedIn
- 32% — a recruiter or headhunter reached out directly
- 21% — personal network / referral
- 21% — applied directly on the company's own site
- 11% — Indeed
- 6% — Net-Empregos
- 6% — SAPO Emprego
- 1% — ITJobs

That's not a comfortable read if you're building a new job board. LinkedIn dominates overwhelmingly, and even the largest general-purpose platforms in Portugal (Net-Empregos, SAPO) barely register as a real hiring source for IT. We're not going to pretend that isn't a real problem — it's the single biggest obstacle any new platform has to overcome, and it's a big part of why so much of our work (SEO, content, this article included) is about giving people a reason to know us before they actively need to job hunt.

## The number one frustration, by a wide margin

We asked people to check off everything that frustrates them about job hunting in Portugal. One answer stood out clearly above the rest:

**74%** — salaries are never published in job ads.

Next came 63% citing too many intermediary recruiters with no direct employer contact, 45% citing irrelevant or low-quality offers, and 39% citing companies that post jobs and then never respond.

There's no way around it: salary transparency isn't a "nice to have" we invented for marketing. It's, measurably, the single biggest source of frustration for IT job seekers in Portugal — bigger than being ghosted, bigger than listing quality, bigger than recruiter noise.

## What happens if salary is always visible

We asked directly: if a platform always published the salary range on every listing, would that change your behavior?

- 53% — would use it more, but not exclusively
- 31% — would switch to using it as their primary platform
- 13% — wouldn't change much
- 3% — would be skeptical the ranges were accurate

Combining the first two, **84%** say salary transparency would genuinely change their behavior in favor of the platform offering it. That's the single strongest number backing the rule Just IT is built around — but worth noting the third line too: 13% say it wouldn't change much. Salary transparency doesn't fix everything on its own.

## The part that doesn't flatter us

Here's the uncomfortable result, and we'd rather be upfront about it than bury it: we asked whether people would pay a fee for a premium profile that gets seen first by employers.

**61% said no** — they expect job boards to be free for candidates. Another 24% said "depends on the platform." Only 15% said yes, and even that group capped out at small amounts (up to €5 or €10 a month).

That doesn't validate a candidate-pays model — and to be clear, that was never Just IT's model. Job posting is paid for by companies; candidate access is, and stays, free. But it's a result worth sharing honestly: if we'd been considering monetizing premium candidate profiles, this data told us plainly not to.

## Satisfaction with current options: neither bad nor good

We asked for a 1-5 satisfaction rating on the job search options available to IT professionals in Portugal. The average came out at 2.9 — below the midpoint. 42% gave it a neutral 3, and only 25% gave it a 4 or 5. Not a crisis, but not a market people consider well served either.

## What this actually means

Three things come out of these numbers clearly, and not all of them are what we'd have liked to hear:

Salary transparency is, by a wide margin, the thing people want most and value most — that directly confirms the single most important decision behind Just IT (no job gets published without a salary range, no exceptions).

LinkedIn is, and will probably stay for a while, the dominant channel — no new platform changes that overnight, and it would be naive to promise otherwise.

Candidates don't want to pay, and they're right not to — the correct model is companies paying to post jobs, not candidates paying to be seen.
