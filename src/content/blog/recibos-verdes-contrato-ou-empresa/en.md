Sooner or later, every IT professional in Portugal asks themselves this question: employment contract, recibos verdes, or open a company? The answer you hear most often, "recibos verdes or a company always pays more", isn't always true. We ran the real numbers, using the same calculation engine behind our [net salary calculator](/salario-liquido), for three concrete figures: €2,000, €3,500 and €5,000.

Before the numbers, a note on what this isn't: it's not tax advice, and the three options aren't only about money. Recibos verdes and your own company give you freedom to work with international clients, deduct real expenses, and set your own schedule. An employment contract gives you paid holidays, sick pay, unemployment eligibility, and notice before termination. This is only about what actually lands in your account at the end of the month, under clearly stated assumptions explained below.

## The three regimes, briefly

**Employment contract**: 11% goes to Social Security, IRS is withheld per the official tables, and you're entitled to a meal allowance that's tax-exempt up to €10.46/day (on a card). We have a [full guide](/guia/contrato-trabalho) on how this works.

**Recibos verdes (independent worker)**: you pay 21.4% Social Security on 70% of what you invoice, plus IRS withholding at 23% (or 11.5% depending on activity type). The first year is exempt from Social Security. Our [complete guide](/guia/recibos-verdes) covers how this works in practice.

**Your own company (Sociedade Unipessoal Lda)**: the company pays corporate tax (15% or 19% depending on profit), the manager has a mandatory minimum Social Security base even on a low salary, and distributed dividends are taxed at 28%. Real fixed costs: an accountant, and usually more admin. See the [company guide](/guia/empresa-propria).

## The numbers, side by side

We calculated for a single person with no dependents, resident in mainland Portugal, programming activity (art. 151º), a Portuguese client, no VAT exemption, already past their first 2 years of activity (so no first-year benefits). For the company scenario, we assumed a manager salary of €1,000/month with the rest distributed as dividends, the most tax-efficient strategy for most cases at this income level.

| Monthly gross / invoiced | Employment contract (net, with meal allowance) | Recibos verdes (real net) | Own company (monthly equivalent) |
|---|---|---|---|
| €2,000 | **€1,708.74** | €1,482.77 | €1,195.68 |
| €3,500 | **€2,487.54** | €2,381.42 | €2,097.48 |
| €5,000 | **€3,227.19** | €3,138.78 | €2,999.28 |

The first thing that stands out: the employment contract wins at all three levels, not just competes. That cuts against the widespread assumption that recibos verdes or a company automatically pay more. In these three examples, they don't, at least not in pure monthly net terms.

## Why this happens

There are two concrete reasons, not a coincidence and not always the case:

The meal card allowance is tax-exempt up to €10.46/day: that's roughly €230/month landing clean in an employee's account, with no direct equivalent for recibos verdes or a company (you can declare real expenses, but that's a different thing and has to be justified).

A company carries fixed costs that don't scale down: the accountant costs the same whether you bill €2,000 or €20,000, and corporate tax plus 28% dividend tax amounts to real double taxation. That's why the company option falls clearly behind at €2,000/month (€1,195.68) but gets much closer to the contract at €5,000/month (€2,999.28 vs €3,227.19): fixed costs matter less and less as revenue grows.

## Where this changes

These numbers assume one specific scenario. They change meaningfully if:

You have genuine, significant business expenses (equipment, co-working, training): on recibos verdes or through a company, you can deduct them; on a contract, you can't. This can flip the comparison.

You invoice clients outside Portugal: foreign clients typically don't withhold tax at source, which changes how the money actually arrives (better monthly cash flow, but a tax bill to settle the following year).

You're in your first year on recibos verdes: exempt from Social Security for 12 months, which changes the numbers significantly for that first year only.

You bill meaningfully more than €5,000/month: the gap keeps narrowing, and your own company can actually overtake the other two past a certain point, especially if you retain profit in the company instead of distributing all of it.

## What to take from this

There's no universal answer, and any article telling you to "always pick X" is oversimplifying. What we do know, with real numbers: at the income range most common among IT professionals in Portugal (€2,000–€5,000), a standard employment contract frequently leaves more net cash in your account every month than the alternatives, the opposite of what a lot of people assume.

That doesn't mean you should turn down a recibos verdes project or give up on opening a company: it means it's worth running the numbers for your actual situation before deciding, instead of assuming "independent always pays more."
